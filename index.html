THIS ISN'T A PROMPT-HACK COURSE

The useful skill isn't asking AI more questions. It's knowing what to ask, what to check, and what stays yours to decide.

DEFINITION & CONTEXT

What is AI Ready Colombo?

AI Ready Colombo is a short AI literacy pilot built around practical use rather than technical theory. It focuses on everyday decisions learners face when using generative AI for study, job search, research, writing and work.

The course uses one consistent idea throughout: AI can help with speed, structure and options, but important claims, sensitive information and high-stakes decisions still need human judgment.

COMPARE THE ROLE

AI as an assistant vs AI as the authority

The safer pattern is to use AI to support a task, then keep verification and consequential decisions with a person who understands the context.

Examples of where AI can help and where human oversight still matters
TaskUseful AI roleHuman responsibility
BrainstormingGenerate options, examples and alternative angles.Choose what fits the real goal and audience.
WritingOutline, critique, shorten or improve a draft.Own the facts, tone, meaning and final wording.
ResearchMap questions and suggest what to investigate.Open sources, verify claims and check dates and context.
High-stakes decisionsExplain concepts or help compare considerations.Keep final authority with qualified people and accountable processes.

ONE PRACTICAL SYSTEM

Define. Prompt. Inspect. Verify. Decide.

Use this five-step checklist whenever an AI output matters enough that you would be uncomfortable getting it wrong.

01

Define

Be clear about the real task, audience and stakes before opening an AI tool.

02

Prompt

Give useful context, a clear task, constraints and the output format you need.

03

Inspect

Read the answer critically instead of treating polished wording as proof.

04

Verify

Check important facts, statistics, quotations, links and current information.

05

Decide

Use your own judgment about what to accept, change, reject or escalate.

WHY AI LITERACY MATTERS

The skills around AI are changing quickly.

AI literacy is not only about operating a tool. It also includes judgment, verification, privacy awareness and the ability to keep learning as tools change.

39%

Employers expect 39% of workers' core skills to change by 2030.

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 surveyed more than 1,000 employers across 55 economies. It also identifies AI and big data, technological literacy and analytical thinking among skills expected to grow in importance.
